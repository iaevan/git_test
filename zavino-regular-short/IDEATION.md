# Smart Review & Customer Tracking for restaurants & cafés — ideation

> **Status: not built yet.** This is an upcoming product from **zavino. labs** (next to `forma.` — available to try — and `outbid.bd` — work in progress). Everything here, and the film, says *we're building this*: UI shown on screen is a concept preview, numbers are sample data, and the end card says *coming soon*.

## 1. The one-line idea

**Every table becomes a customer you know.** A QR code on the table earns a verified phone number, a real order and a real bill — then turns that into reviews, regulars and repeat visits.

## 2. Who it's for, and what hurts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Owner / manager of a café or restaurant** (Dhaka first: Dhanmondi, Gulshan, Banani, Bashundhara) | Hundreds of customers a week, zero names. Can't tell regulars from one-offs, can't see big spenders, can't tell why people never return. |
| **The pain nobody sees** | Unhappy customers leave in silence, then post one star. The owner finds out a week late — after the damage. |
| **The money leak** | No customer data → no targeted offers → ad spend on strangers instead of winning back people who already walked in. |

## 4. Working name

Labs names are one word + a dot. Shortlist:

| name | why | risk |
|---|---|---|
| **`regular.`** ← used in the film | the thing every café wants to create; reads well in a sentence ("Rafi's a regular.") | common word, hard to trademark |
| `usual.` | "The usual, Rafi?" is the best moment in the story | softer meaning on its own |
| `encore.` | return visits | generic, crowded name |
| `tabletalk.` | the QR-on-the-table origin | long |

It is one constant in the scenes (`NAME`) — swapping it means re-rendering the end shots.

## 5. Film concepts considered

1. **"The Quiet Walk-out"** — owner POV, pure pain (silent customer leaves → one-star). Strong hook, but no product.
2. **"Rafi's Tuesday"** — follow one customer through every feature. Clear, but reads like a feature tour.
3. **"The Usual"** ← chosen. Open on the owner's blind spot (hundreds of strangers), twist into the product via Rafi's visit — your Dhanmondi example, one chicken burger and a cold coffee, ৳850 — then pay it off: weeks later the owner greets him with **"The usual, Rafi?"**. Pain → product → emotional payoff → "coming soon".

## 7. Characters

* **Nila** — owner of *Café 27* (Dhanmondi, Road 27). Forest-green apron, hair in a bun. Tired → relieved → delighted.
* **Rafi** — the customer. Terracotta hoodie, cap. Easy-going; the one who becomes a regular.
* **Everyone else** — soft, faceless sand/sage shapes. They get a name tag and a "?" before the product, a profile after.

## 8. Fun beats (the polish list)

* the scan frame snapping onto the QR; OTP boxes filling; a cash-register *ka-ching* on ৳850; a stamp-thunk for **VERIFIED** and **REGULAR**
* low rating buzzes Nila's phone — she's at Rafi's table with a free coffee before it becomes a review

## 9. What we don't claim

* No fake logos, no real-looking review sites, no invented customer numbers presented as fact — screens carry a *concept preview* tag and use sample data.
* POS integration is "where possible"; otherwise the cashier verifies by hand (the film shows the manual path).
* WhatsApp/Google are named only as channels the owner picks, not as endorsements.

## 10. Product backlog this film implies (for the build)

1. Table QR + offer landing page · OTP phone verify · 24-hour session
2. Order picker from the restaurant's menu · one-time discount code (time-limited, one per number per visit)
3. Cashier screen: code check + bill entry (manual first, POS connector later)
4. Post-meal review request (WhatsApp/SMS) · private rating · Google review link for happy customers · instant low-rating alert
5. Customer profile & segments (regulars, big spenders, lapsed) · owner-set offer rules (first visit, 5th visit)
6. AI campaign studio (festival templates: Eid, Ramadan, Pohela Boishakh) with approval gate · upload-your-own creatives
7. Campaign attribution ("how many came back?") · monthly report
